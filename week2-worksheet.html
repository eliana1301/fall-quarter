2Ask an Alum READING

Read for gist: would Ji-woo recommend Massachusetts? Then notice the verbs in bold.

Read: Ask an Alum

Every month, we interview a former Approach student. This month: Ji-woo Park from Busan, South Korea, who now works as a nurse in Worcester.

Q: How long have you lived in Massachusetts?

I have lived here for almost four years. I arrived in January 2023, and two days later a huge snowstorm closed the whole city. Welcome to New England!

Q: Was it hard at first?

Very. During my first month, I didn't understand anyone at the coffee shop. Then I studied at Approach for eight months, and my English improved a lot. I still remember my first presentation. My hands shook the whole time.

Q: What has changed since then?

Everything! I have finished nursing school, I have moved twice, and I have made friends from all over the world. I have also learned to love the winter... well, almost.

Q: Have you ever thought about going back to Korea?

Of course. I went back last summer for three weeks to see my family. It was wonderful, but after two weeks I missed my apartment, my coworkers, and even the T.

Q: Have you traveled around New England?

A little. I have been to Maine and Vermont, but I haven't visited Rhode Island yet, which is crazy because it's so close. My favorite trip was to Acadia National Park. We went in October 2023 and watched the sunrise from Cadillac Mountain.

Q: What advice do you have for new students?

Talk to people. When I arrived, I was too shy, and I lost a lot of time. My roommate Ana has just started at Approach, and I told her the same thing yesterday: don't wait until your English is perfect.

Q: Last question. Has Massachusetts changed you?

Definitely. I have become more independent. I have also become a Red Sox fan, which my family doesn't understand at all.

Would Ji-woo recommend Massachusetts to a new student?
Discussion questions for your group
  1. Would Ji-woo recommend Massachusetts to a new student? How do you know?
  2. Ji-woo says she lost time because she was shy. Do you agree with her advice?
  3. Which of Ji-woo's experiences have you also had? Which haven't you had yet?

5What's the Difference? SPOT THE DIFFERENCE

In pairs, explain the difference in meaning. Then answer the question.

1

A Marco lived in Boston for two years.

B Marco has lived in Boston for two years.

Who still lives in Boston?
B. A = Marco left Boston (finished period). B = he still lives there.
2

A Have you seen Professor Lee this morning?

B Did you see Professor Lee this morning?

One person asks at 10 a.m., the other at 3 p.m. Who is who?
A = 10 a.m. (the morning isn't over). B = 3 p.m. (the morning is finished).
3

A Sofia has gone to Cape Cod.

B Sofia has been to Cape Cod.

Who is on Cape Cod right now?
A. Gone = she's there now or on her way. Been = she went and came back. (This contrast is clearest in British English; Americans sometimes say has gone to for an experience.)
4

A I've lost my student ID.

B I lost my student ID last week.

Who still can't get into the building?
A. The present perfect shows the result now. With B, the loss is a finished event; maybe they found it or got a new one.
5

A Have you ever met a famous person?

B Did you ever meet your grandfather?

Why does B use the simple past?
The grandfather probably died, so that time period is finished. A = your life up to now.
6

A It's the first time I've tried clam chowder.

B It was the first time I tried clam chowder.

Who is eating clam chowder right now?
A. It's the first time + present perfect = now. It was the first time + simple past = a finished moment.

Your partner has the information you need. Ask questions to fill your five gaps. Don't look at your partner's text!

How to choose the right question
  • Finished date or event? → When did...?
  • Still true now? → How long has/have...?
  • Up to now? → How many... has/have...?
